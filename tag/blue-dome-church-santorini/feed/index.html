Geographical and Cultural Context of the Island

Santorini is part of the Cyclades archipelago and is located approximately 120 miles southeast of mainland Greece. Its unique geographical features are the result of volcanic activity that shaped the island’s current landscape. The crescent-shaped island is formed by the caldera, a submerged volcanic crater, creating a natural harbor surrounded by towering cliffs.

The culture of the island is deeply rooted in Greek heritage. Its warm hospitality and lively spirit welcome visitors from all corners of the world. Santorini’s locals take pride in preserving their heritage, evident in the island’s traditional festivals, delicious cuisine, and warm community atmosphere. Exploring the quaint villages, such as Oia, Fira, and Pyrgos, allows visitors to immerse themselves in the local culture and experience the authentic charm of Santorini.
